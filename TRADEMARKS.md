# Trademarks

The code in this repository is MIT licensed. The names and the logos are not.

Copyright and trademark are separate things, and the MIT licence only speaks to
the first. It grants the right to use, copy, modify and sell the code. It grants
nothing at all in the name a product is sold under, or in the marks that appear
in `assets/`. Nothing here restricts what the MIT licence permits; it states what
the MIT licence was never granting.

## What a fork may and may not do

A fork may take the code, change it, and sell it. That is what MIT allows and
that is deliberate.

A fork may not keep the Crowdlinker name or logo on it, and may not present
itself in a way that suggests Crowdlinker built it, endorsed it, supports it, or
stands behind it. Rename it, and take the Crowdlinker logo out of the banner and
the README.

Saying true things about where a fork came from is fine and always will be: a
fork of JevPromptCoach, based on JevPromptCoach, compatible with JevPromptCoach.
The line is between describing a relationship and implying a blessing.

## What this file does not do

It does not register anything. A registered mark comes from a filing with a
trademark office, and no notice in a repository substitutes for one. What use in
commerce gives you is an unregistered claim, which is real but narrower and
harder to enforce.

It is also worth being clear about what is actually defensible here. The plugin
is named after somebody else's model. The distinctive part of `JevPromptCoach` is
Jev, which is TypeSafe's, and "prompt coach" describes what the thing does, which
is the kind of phrase trademark law protects weakly if at all. The mark with real
weight behind it is Crowdlinker. If the plugin name is meant to become an asset
rather than a description, it needs a name that does not borrow its distinctive
half from a vendor.
